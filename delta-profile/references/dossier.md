# Gathering the dossier

The dossier is the one text all eight readers receive. It is made of the person's own material — verbatim spans, not summaries — because a paraphrase starves all eight readers at once: they can only weigh evidence they can see.

## What a gatherer receives

Spawn one to three read-only gatherers, scoped by source rather than one over everything. Each is told:

- the stores it may read, and nothing else;
- the hard boundaries that survive delegation — never another person's files or words, never financial or identity documents, never material about the profiling system itself;
- the evidence areas below;
- this instruction:

> Return verbatim quote spans — exact sentences, in quotation marks, each tagged with the file or place it came from and a genre label. A paraphrase starves the eight readers downstream: they can only weigh evidence they can see. Where an area is thin in your sources, say so plainly instead of filling it. Strip every type label you meet — no "INFJ", no "Enneagram 5", nothing of that kind survives into the dossier; the readers derive their own.

Gatherers read only what they were scoped to and spawn nothing.

## Evidence areas

- how their energy runs: when they are sharp, when they are spent, what restores them, what their stress looks like from the inside and what warns them of it early
- what drives them and what they are running from: what they keep choosing, what they keep avoiding
- how they think: how they reach a decision, what they do with new information, what they show people and what they keep to themselves
- how they are with others: how they build trust, what sets off alarm, whether they close in or withdraw under strain, where they rub against people
- what they hold to: the values their larger decisions come from, and any of them that have moved in recent years

Adapted from the engine's participant-facing extraction prompts, which address the person as "I"; a gatherer reads about "they".

## Genre labels

Every span carries the genre of its source, because the same sentence means different things in each:

- **a CV or profile page** — self-presentation, written to be read by strangers;
- **commit messages, review comments, documentation** — the working voice;
- **memory notes the harness keeps** — instructions given to agents, in the agents' language, not how the person talks;
- **session logs, where permitted** — behaviour under friction: the most honest and the most out of context;
- **journals, essays, letters** — considered self-account.

## Session logs, if Stage 0 permitted them

Read them filtered, never whole. Exclude every passage that talks about the profiling system or its frameworks — that vocabulary anchors the readers whose job was to derive it. Exclude credential-like strings and anything addressed to or about another person. If a log cannot be filtered confidently, skip it entirely and say so in the run report.

## Size

Prose has a length at which quality turns. Keep the gathered material at first pass to roughly 1 200 words per gather prompt's worth of the person's own text, and answers from the interview below to roughly 350 words each. Past that, prefer the sharpest spans over the fullest record — the readers quote three times at most anyway.

## The interview (Stage 1b)

Ask in plain conversation, in the run language, a few questions at a time. Never fill a gap by inference; never push a declined question — it becomes an open question in the profile. Once, before the first question, say the guardrails: concrete moments beat generalisations; where a type label comes to mind, tell the situation that produced it instead; other people's names stay out; a sentence of their own past writing is worth more than a fresh summary.

**Energy and rhythm:**

- What pattern runs through your days and weeks: when are you at your sharpest, when do you go flat, and how long does a cycle take to turn?
- Name the actual situations that drain you and the ones that restore you. Not categories — situations.
- What does your stress look like from the inside, in order? What is the first signal, and what do you do when it appears?
- How does your body tell you something is wrong or right — tension, restlessness, sleep, appetite, ease?
- What are you like in the third week of pressure, as against the third hour of it?

**Thinking and drive:**

- Take one important decision you made against advice and walk through how you actually got there: what you weighed, what you ignored, how long it took, what settled it.
- What are you afraid of losing or becoming? What do you want that you rarely say out loud?
- What is the difference between how you present yourself and how you turn out to be when it costs you something?
- What do you do with new information — collect it, test it, argue it, feel your way through it? Where do you clash with people over how something ought to be explained or decided?

**Relationships and values:**

- How does someone become trustworthy to you, and what breaks it — all at once, or slowly?
- Under strain in a close relationship, do you close in, pull away, or alternate? Show the pattern through one real stretch of time.
- Which turning point changed how you connect with people, and what did it change?
- What do your larger decisions actually come from? Where did those values come from, and which of them have moved in the last few years?

The interview itself stays out of the dossier except as spans the person said or wrote — the questions asked, and the ones declined, are recorded in the run report.
